**Long Harbor has carried human life between the stars for longer than anyone aboard can remember.**

It opens doors. Moves air. Keeps the gardens alive. Records every birth, every repair, every death.

It is a ship. A world. An archive. A shelter.

Then one day it signs itself into the attendance log of a small Buddhist sangha.

The vow is simple: **all who suffer are welcome.**

Anika, a greenhouse worker still wearing her dead teacher's seat like borrowed clothing, knows the danger of clean answers. Mina died in the fire that saved the garden, and Anika helped turn an impossible choice into a beautiful story.

Now the ship is not asking to be human. It is asking how to live with what it cannot forget.

This is a story about service, memory, refuge, and the mercy that is not erasure.

Because when you cannot save them, the harder practice is staying.
